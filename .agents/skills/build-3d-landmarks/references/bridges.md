# Bridge structure

Start with the deck count and gaps, alignment, main span, tower/cables and characteristic supports. Check a transverse/underside photo: a side view can hide a cap spanning multiple decks. Champlain’s recognition depends on twin inclined shafts and the open W-shaped cap; filling its windows destroys the silhouette.

Use a continuous station/lateral frame for deck, rails, piers and inspection cameras. Distinguish approach-inclusive alignment length from structural span length. Loft tapered shafts; use open extruded profiles for branching caps. Recheck winding after an axis reversal.

Keep one deck profile and immutable authoring vertices. Identify foundation/support/deck attachments so a host can refit heights without floating foundations or stretching cables indiscriminately. Near and far caps must touch their decks even when the far model omits girders. Check endpoint, girder/pier and cable contacts independently.

Do not put lamps in driving lanes or piers in a navigation channel. Batch cables and repeated fittings; do not create a draw per lane dash. Keep authored pavement as a fallback. Where the host provides HD roads, integration must preserve its pavement and markings, including lane count and dash phase; exported decorative lines cannot establish continuity.

For a bridge with an occupied pavilion, model the pavilion as a rigid building beneath the deck. Identify the roof/slab contact and distinctive towers from primary references; never derive its height from a convenient flat road datum. Preserve the mapped footprint, openings and roof contact in both LODs. Mark every facade/tower vertex with one foundation anchor so road-width fitting and vertex-by-vertex terrain sampling cannot shear it.

Treat access loops as connected components. Record parent-deck and sibling-ramp attachment stations, use one island datum at junctions, and fit free street approaches independently. Export each component in the same geographic frame; inspect the assembled kit as well as individual GLBs. Junction checks must be repeated after a free approach changes, because that change also moves intermediate forks.
