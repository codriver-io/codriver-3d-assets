# Bridge structure

Start with the deck count and gaps, alignment, main span, tower/cables and characteristic supports. Check a transverse/underside photo: a side view can hide a cap spanning multiple decks. Champlain’s recognition depends on twin inclined shafts and the open W-shaped cap; filling its windows destroys the silhouette.

Use a continuous station/lateral frame for deck, rails, piers and inspection cameras. Distinguish approach-inclusive alignment length from structural span length. Loft tapered shafts; use open extruded profiles for branching caps. Recheck winding after an axis reversal.

After rotating a 2D shape into place, check the resulting lateral side numerically: `rotateX(-π/2)` maps the shape's +y to −z, so an overlook drawn outward can land on the sidewalk. Overlook and balcony floors lie under their parapets, flush with the walk, built with their ledge as one rigid unit across alignment bends. Width, edge and profile functions are continuous across every branch join and fork: sample them across each join (no pinch, no snap), because pavement, walkways and the host's road heights all read the same edges. Cap every girder, slab and fill at its exposed ends only; an open end shows the hollow box from beside the abutment.

Measure deck, carriageway and raised-walk widths from attributed aerial imagery (the national orthophoto service where available), rather than lane-width guesses. Rigid steel decks retain authored width during lateral fitting; fragmentary live pavement samples cannot squeeze a two-way deck. Keep one deck profile and immutable authoring vertices. Identify foundation/support/deck attachments so a host can refit heights without floating foundations or stretching cables indiscriminately. Near and far caps must touch their decks even when the far model omits girders. Every cable/stay/hanger/rail/walkway/beam end meets an anchor, saddle, deck, pier or ground in both LODs. Test named terminal contacts, excluding the member and including a shortened-end negative case; visible free ends are FIX even if documented. Check endpoint, girder/pier and cable contacts independently.

Do not put lamps in driving lanes or piers in a navigation channel. Batch cables and repeated fittings; do not create a draw per lane dash. Keep authored pavement as a fallback. Where the host provides HD roads, integration must preserve its pavement and markings, including lane count and dash phase; exported decorative lines cannot establish continuity. Authored approaches over ordinary streets must bury conflicting live paint. If a host fits bank height only at the centreline, inspect full-width joints on sloped quays and report lateral height mismatch rather than declaring a wide deck verified.

For a bridge with an occupied pavilion, model the pavilion as a rigid building beneath the deck. Identify the roof/slab contact and distinctive towers from primary references; never derive its height from a convenient flat road datum. Preserve the mapped footprint, openings and roof contact in both LODs. Mark every facade/tower vertex with one foundation anchor so road-width fitting and vertex-by-vertex terrain sampling cannot shear it.

Treat access loops as connected components. Record parent-deck and sibling-ramp attachment stations, use one island datum at junctions, and fit free street approaches independently. Export each component in the same geographic frame; inspect the assembled kit as well as individual GLBs. Junction checks must be repeated after a free approach changes, because that change also moves intermediate forks.
