# Pont Pierre-Laporte (`pont-pierre-laporte`)

The suspension bridge carrying Autoroute 73 over the St. Lawrence between Sainte-Foy (Québec) and Lévis, opened in November 1970: Canada's longest main span, in its current state with six lanes and a median barrier, no sidewalks. The Pont de Québec (1917 cantilever, Route 175 and rail) stands 200 m downstream; it is a separate landmark and is not drawn or claimed here. A **road-fitted bridge**: the deck, car, route, camera and HD pavement share one station/lateral/height profile built on the mapped OSM carriageways.

Files: `src/peregrine/landmarks/quebec/pont-pierre-laporte/` (`config.js`, `footprint.js`, `geometry.js`, `views.js`, `layer.js`, `pont-pierre-laporte-profile.js`, `-structure.js`, `-alignment.js` generated by `-derive.mjs`, `pont-pierre-laporte.test.js`), `prototypes/assets3d/catalog.d/pont-pierre-laporte.json`, exports `public/models/bridges/pont-pierre-laporte{-near,-far}.glb` + `.json`. Build: `pnpm build:quebec-landmarks pont-pierre-laporte`.

## Modelling

- **Towers**: two cruciform legs lofted between the pier pedestals and the leg tops (grooves near only), the top portal with its round-arched soffit, the deck-level strut with its shallow arched soffit (the trusses rest on it), saddle housings on the leg tops and one red obstruction light per leg. Pale cream (`tower`).
- **Deck**: the two grey Warren trusses run continuously from anchorage to anchorage, passing between the legs; bottom lateral bracing and floor-beam struts (near); steel floor, concrete traffic barriers at the kerbs, service strips, the outer railing on the top chords, median barrier, lamp standards with the head over the road every 45.6 m (near). Cables and suspenders in a darker grey (`cable`).
- **Anchorages**: stepped concrete blocks under the road at both clifftops (pilaster relief near) with cable housings beside the road that the cables run into just above the railing.
- **Approaches**: beyond the anchorages, a concrete deck with parapets on a box girder and two-column piers, on fill below 8 m, pavement on grade at the feet; the two pavements follow the mapped carriageways where they drift apart (up to ~16 m between centres).
- **Materials** (8): `tower`, `steel`, `cable`, `concrete`, `asphalt` (recoloured to the map pavement at run time), `paint`, `lamp` and `light` (self-lit).
- **Far LOD** (2-8 km): same bounds; legs without grooves, portals and struts with fewer segments, 5-sided cables (0.45 m so they still read), every other suspender, truss chords and diagonals every other panel, no verticals, bracing, barriers, lamps or paint.

## Flat Cityscape convention and road ownership

Cityscape has no river and no cliffs. The deck is level at 54.0 m between the anchorages and eases (smoothstep) to the loaded approach roads (`'start'`/`'end'`) over **800 m at each end**, steepest grade **10.1 %**: north over the Henri-IV road towards the Duplessis interchange, south over the Marie-Victorin viaduct and the Pont Risi on Autoroute Robert-Cliche. Station frame: s north to south, lateral + west (southbound). `roadEdgesAt(s)` keeps `ROAD_EDGES` (±0.3 to ±10.95 m) on the mapped bridge and follows each mapped carriageway ±6 m beyond it (blended over 60 m), the Golden Gate Bridge's method; no shared code was changed for it.

The Pont de Québec's Route 175 deck runs parallel 200 m downstream: every Route 175 vertex projects 150 m or more from the alignment, far outside the 26 m ownership corridor (`bridge-roads.js`: half width 14 m + 12 m margin), so the road layer cannot claim it; the test pins this.

## Full 3D world: the real deck

`terrainPolicy: 'absolute-deck'` (the Golden Gate's shared policy): no corridor flattening, the structure keeps its authored heights above sea level, weight-0 supports (pier footings, anchorage bases, approach piers and fill) reach their own DEM, and the ramp ends meet the terrain roads. The flat-map 800 m ramps are wrong on real terrain: the road leaves both clifftops at about deck level. `layer.js` therefore tags the terrain surface (`realSurface`, the Bay Bridge East Span's method) and the profile returns the **real deck** there: level at 54.0 m from the north anchorage to 40 m before the end of the Marie-Victorin viaduct, and on the approaches over the ground (`REAL_APPROACH`, knots every 10 m, by a monotone cubic), ending on the terrain roads. One GLB serves both modes.

The first real profile went 1.0 m over the highest of five Terrarium samples across the deck every 20 m. On the DEM that gave grades up to 19 % (15.5 % over the Henri-IV road's first 100 m, 19 % climbing a DEM hump south of the viaduct where the real road runs in a cut), the northbound lane 0.79 m below its ground at the south end (the deck met the centreline DEM, the carriageways 13 m apart on a 1.8 m cross-slope) with the southbound lane 0.99 m above its own, and terrain through the deck where the south ramp starts its descent towards the Pont Risi (the northbound outer edge 0.67 m under the DEM at 2,242 m). Now (`tmp/quebec/pont-pierre-laporte/envelope.mjs`, `smooth.mjs`, worktree scratch):

- **Minimum clearance**: Terrarium samples every 5 m at both carriageways' centres and edges and 1 m beyond their outer edges (`roadEdgesAt`); the target is the higher of the first profile and the highest sample + 1.1 m (outside the last 25 m at each end, which blend into the terrain roads).
- **Smoothing**: the upper envelope of that target with grades limited to 8 %, averaged over 20 m, sampled every 10 m. Steepest real grade **8.9 %** (was 19 %); the deck stays at least 0.7 m over every sample and never below the first profile by more than 0.1 m; it rides up to 3.2 m over the first profile across the DEM humps south of the viaduct (where the real road is in a cut: the deck stands on its approach structure there instead of climbing the hill).
- **Lane-wise lift** (`layer.js`, Full 3D world only; no shared code changed): the real deck ends at the DEM on the alignment's centreline ('start'/'end'), so over the last 400 m before each end the deck tilts across, a plane through the two carriageway centres, until each centre meets its own ground (measured from the DEM at run time: +0.87 m northbound, −0.89 m southbound at the south end, ~0 at the north end). The geometry (after every fit), the HD pavement overlays and `heightAt` (so the car, route and camera) all carry it. The blend is long so that the twist it puts into the deck's 12.5 m quads stays inside the 6 cm between the pavement and the concrete slab under it (60 m and 150 m blends showed the slab through the median in diamonds).

## Approximations and not modelled

The tower positions are estimated (no tower or pier is mapped; centring the main span on the mapped bridge puts both at the shorelines, consistent with photographs and the DEM). Railings and barriers are solid; suspender pairs are one ribbon; the transverse trusses every 6 m are drawn as floor-beam struts every 15 m; gusset plates, cable bands, inspection gantries, signage, drainage and the anchorages' real shape inside the cliffs are not modelled; on the flat map the anchorages stand 53 m tall from grade. Lane paint is a fallback that the HD paint replaces.
