# Pont de Québec (`pont-de-quebec`)

The Quebec Bridge over the St. Lawrence between Sainte-Foy (Québec) and Lévis: the 1917 steel cantilever bridge, opened to rail in 1919 after the 1907 and 1916 collapses, still the world's longest cantilever span. Today it carries Route 175 (three lanes, the middle one reversible), one CN/VIA track and a walkway. The Pont Pierre-Laporte (A-73) stands ~200 m upstream; it is a separate landmark and is not drawn or claimed here. A **road-fitted bridge**: the deck, car, route, camera and HD pavement share one station/lateral/height profile built on the mapped Route 175 roadway. The track and walkway are geometry only.

Files: `src/peregrine/landmarks/quebec/pont-de-quebec/` (`config.js`, `footprint.js`, `geometry.js`, `views.js`, `layer.js`, `pont-de-quebec-profile.js`, `-structure.js`, `-alignment.js` generated by `-derive.mjs`, `pont-de-quebec.test.js`), `prototypes/assets3d/catalog.d/pont-de-quebec.json`, exports `public/models/bridges/pont-de-quebec{-near,-far}.glb` + `.json`. ## Modelling

- **Trusses**: two K-trusses, one open-box member per chord panel, vertical and diagonal (merged per material). The anchor arms rise from the anchor-end pylons to the main posts, the cantilever arms fall to the tips, the suspended span's top chord is a parabola; the bottom chords slope from deck level at the anchor ends and tips down to the pin shoes, and a deck-level member runs through the arms in each truss plane (the floor line lit in night photographs).
- **Bracing**: transverse sway frames at every panel point above the road (strut at 7.5 m and X to the top chord, near only), heavier portals at the main posts, tips and anchor ends (the anchor-end portal a deep cross girder between the pylons, as seen from the road), X bracing below the deck where the chords dip, top and bottom laterals (near).
- **Deck**: steel floor between the trusses; Route 175 asphalt, a concrete wall against the track with lamp standards over the road, a kerb and the raised walkway with two railings inside the east truss; the track (ballast band, two rails near) near the centre line; a railing inside the west truss.
- **Masonry** (`stone`): main piers with cutwaters up- and downstream, granite pedestals and steel shoes; tall anchor-pier towers under each truss joined by a lower wall, with a cornice; one approach pier each end; full-width clifftop abutments.
- **Beyond the abutments**: only Route 175 continues (concrete deck with parapets on a box girder and two-column piers, fill near the feet); the track and walkway end at the abutments.
- **Materials** (9): `steel` (dark charcoal-brown, linear 0.07/0.05/0.04, sRGB #4b3f38) and `stone` (dark grey granite, linear 0.16/0.15/0.13, #6f6c65), both much darker than the first pass's grey steel and beige stone (the photographs and the refs sheet show dark steel and granite); the dark theme is scaled down by the same factors (#484d50, #454645), `floor`, `concrete`, `asphalt` (recoloured to the map pavement at run time), `rail`, `paint`, `lamp` and `light` (self-lit; red obstruction lights on the main posts).
- **Far LOD** (2-8 km): same bounds; the chords merged into one member per straight run (the suspended span's curved top chord stays panel by panel), every other vertical, one full-depth diagonal per panel instead of the K (alternating direction), the deck-level floor line merged per run, four-panel approach trusses (all 1.3× thicker), portals at the main posts, tips and anchor ends; no sway frames, laterals, rails, railings, lamps or paint.

## Flat Cityscape convention and road ownership

Cityscape has no river and no cliffs. The road is level at 48.0 m between the mapped bridge ends and ramps to the loaded approach roads (`'start'`/`'end'`), a constant grade between a 24 m parabolic vertical curve at the bridge end and a 12 m one at the foot (`rampShape`). Station frame: s north to south along the roadway's centreline, lateral + west.

Both ends of Route 175 sit in interchanges on the clifftops, where the real road is at grade. The first version ramped 560 m north (9.8 %) and 480 m south (11.7 %), so six mapped links met the raised ramp: a car on them jumped up or dropped in the last metres (heights then: the on-ramp 457 m north 6.7 m, the exit to Boulevard Champlain 297 m north 22.4 m, the northbound branch of Boulevard Guillaume-Couture 93 m south 41.2 m, the link from the Route Marie-Victorin loop 241 m south 23.8 m, the loop itself 319 m south 14.7 m, the exit to Route 132 397 m south 5.6 m). The ramps are now placed by the mapped junctions (`links`, `joins`, `over`, `under` in the alignment, from the 2026-10-04 extract), the Pont Laviolette's and Papineau-Leblanc's method:

- **North, 258 m** (`RAMP_NORTH`), steepest **20.0 %**: the foot is just south of Boulevard Champlain's overpass (OSM 103953555, bridge, layer 1, over Route 175 262-278 m from the bridge end: `OVER_S`), so Route 175 passes under it at grade (HD draws the overpass 5 m up), and the exit to Boulevard Champlain (1561827256, 297 m) and the on-ramp (1206717133, 457 m) leave and join the provider road at grade.
- **South, 241 m** (`RAMP_SOUTH`, ending at `SOUTH_FOOT`, the node where the link from the Marie-Victorin loop joins), steepest **21.5 %**: that link, the loop (319 m) and the exit to Route 132 (397 m) meet the provider road at grade. Route Marie-Victorin passes under 32-50 m before the foot (`UNDER_S`, westbound and eastbound): the box girder stops at a bent 4 m north of it and a girderless slab spans to the fill, its underside at least 4.6 m above both carriageways (8.6 and 6.8 m deck over their centres; test by raycast).
- **The leg** (`LEG`): the northbound branch of Boulevard Guillaume-Couture (25794860, one lane, "175 Nord") joins 93 m from the bridge end, where the ramp is 30.6 m up; no foot position can reach grade there. It gets its own ramp: for 25 m from the junction it lies on the main ramp's surface extended sideways (a car changing between them sees one surface), blends over 25 m into its own profile, eases from the main ramp's grade to **14.1 %** and reaches grade 10 m before its mapped start (the junction with Route Marie-Victorin and the Guillaume-Couture carriageway, at grade). The deck widens east between the junction and the gore (`GORE_OPEN`..`GORE_END`), the east parapet opens there. The leg and the gore are drawn in their own meshes (`pont-de-quebec-leg-asphalt`, `-leg-concrete`): a 7.8 m slab with parapets (near), a girder on single-column piers while high, fill below 3.2 m. `layer.js` masks the provider's flat road under the leg (standard ribbons along the branch, HD pavement on it, up to its foot) while the bridge is active on the flat map.

The trade-off: 20-21.5 % ramps, about twice the 12 % aimed for. The 48 m deck (published clearance) cannot reach grade in less than ~400 m at 12 %, and the first junctions are 297 m (north, with the overpass at 262 m) and 93 m (south) from the bridge ends; a 12 % ramp would leave links on the raised ramp (or need a ramp for each of them in turn: the south loop and its links are 60-120 m long). Visual choice, not survey.

Ownership is Route 175 and, on the flat map, the leg. The provider corridor is the roadway plus 0.9 m (`width` 9.17 m, `ownershipMargin` 0.9: ±5.5 m), so the mapped track (8.0-8.3 m west) and walkway (5.8-6.7 m east) are never captured and stay drawn by the provider; `ownsPoint` limits HD pavement capture to the roadway ±0.8 m; `bridgeRoadHeight` answers within the road edges ±0.6 m, and on the leg within its 3.0 m half-width ±0.6 m for a heading along it, up to its foot (never in Full 3D world). The Pont Pierre-Laporte's A-73 carriageways project 100 m or more from this alignment. No shared code was changed (`ownershipMargin`, `ownsPoint`, the road-mask helpers and the layer subclass are existing extension points).

## Full 3D world: the real deck

`terrainPolicy: 'absolute-deck'` (as the Pont Pierre-Laporte): no corridor flattening, the structure keeps its authored heights above high water, weight-0 supports (main piers, anchor piers, approach piers, abutments, ramp piers and fill) reach their own DEM. `layer.js` tags the terrain surface (`realSurface`) and the profile returns the **real deck**: level at 48.0 m between the bridge ends, and beyond them 0.6 m over the highest of five Terrarium samples across the road (d = -6..+6 m) every 20 m (`REAL_APPROACH`, metres out from each bridge end; `REAL_KNOTS` keeps those inside the shorter ramps), by a monotone cubic, ending on the terrain roads at the new feet (DEM 60.0 m north, 51.9 m south). The leg is not owned and its meshes are hidden: the real branch runs at grade and the provider draws it. At 20 m from the north end the samples climb the DEM's smoothing of the cliff edge (50.0 m centreline, 50.8 m at the west kerb); that knot takes the centreline + 0.5 m (50.5 m) so the road leaves the abutment at ~15 % instead of 23 %. Steepest real grade 19.5 %, 10 m off the south end (the DEM's own cliff edge).

## Approximations and not modelled

The pier positions are estimated (no pier is mapped; centring the main span on the mapped bridge puts the north main pier ~80 m into the river and the south one at the shore). Members are plain boxes: no lacing, eyebars, gussets or pins; the suspended span's hanging connection at the tips is not distinguished; railings are solid bars; the walkway is concrete; lamp spacing, the floor depth and the anchor-end portal shape are estimated; signage, catenary-free track details, the floodlighting and the 1907/1916 memorial are not modelled. On the flat map the abutments and anchor piers stand ~47 m tall from grade, and the ramps (20.0 % and 21.5 %) and the branch's ramp (14.1 %) are much steeper than any real road: the price of meeting every junction at grade. Not checked: the HD half of the branch mask (HD lanes unavailable locally); the branch ramp has no lane paint of its own. Full 3D world does not show Route Marie-Victorin's underpass (the DEM is smooth there and the real deck rides 0.7 m over it). Lane paint is a fallback that the HD paint replaces.
