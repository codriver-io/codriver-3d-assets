# Pont Dubuc (`pont-dubuc`)

The Route 175 crossing of the Saguenay between Chicoutimi and Chicoutimi-Nord (Saguenay), built 1970-1972 and opened in October 1972 to replace the 1933 Pont de Sainte-Anne (the truss bridge 250 m downstream, now for pedestrians and cyclists). **It is an ordinary, functional bridge, not an architectural one**: a 458 m continuous steel box girder in seven spans on seven reinforced-concrete piers, low over the river, with a four-lane deck split by a median wall. It is modelled faithfully as such: a dark green steel box under the middle of a 22.8 m deck, inclined brackets out to fascia girders under the wide overhangs, six pale twin-column river piers whose square columns rise from a battered wall, a concrete end pier at the Chicoutimi end, 13 double-arm lamp posts on the median, a sidewalk behind a barrier and a railed parapet each side. A **road-fitted bridge**: the deck, car, route, camera and HD pavement share one station/lateral/height profile built on the two mapped Route 175 carriageways.

Files: `src/peregrine/landmarks/quebec/pont-dubuc/` (`config.js`, `footprint.js`, `geometry.js`, `views.js`, `layer.js`, `pont-dubuc-profile.js`, `-structure.js`, `-alignment.js` generated by `-derive.mjs`, `pont-dubuc.test.js`), `prototypes/assets3d/catalog.d/pont-dubuc.json`, exports `public/models/bridges/pont-dubuc{-near,-far}.glb` + `.json`. Build: `pnpm build:quebec-landmarks pont-dubuc`. One shared change: an optional `clipStandardEnds` spec flag in `bridge-roads.js` (below).

## Modelling

- **Deck** (`geometry.js`): every layer is a crisp-edged sweep along the profile (pavement, median wall, barriers, sidewalks, parapets, the slab), railings (near) as one solid galvanised panel on the parapet under a top bar, 13 double-arm lamp posts with lamp heads. Lane paint is a fallback the HD paint replaces.
- **Steel**: the trapezoidal box (top buried 2 cm in the slab; it runs on over P1 to the south abutment, as far as the flat-map deck can hold it: the same 1.2 m rule as at the north end), the two fascia girders (outer face inset 2 cm so it is not coplanar with P1's cap beam), and per bracket station an inclined strut from the box's bottom corner to the fascia girder plus a floor beam under the slab: the diagonal rhythm the photographs show under the deck.
- **Piers**: P2-P7 are two column prisms with heads and a battered wall prism, all with a lift weight proportional to height (the pier stretches whole between the river bed and the steel in Full 3D world). P1 is a wall under a deck-wide cap that the steel runs over.
- **Ramps**: beyond the steel the deck rests on solid ramps between retaining walls (base on the ground, `bridgeLift` 0). The pavement's underside floors 4 cm above the slab's at the ramp feet (both reached grade and were coplanar).
- **Chunks**: three station chunks in near; far is one chunk, drops paint, the railing, floor beams and every other bracket, keeps the lamp posts (the silhouette's top).
- Materials: `steel` (dark green), `concrete`, `asphalt`, `paint`, `rail` (railing panel and top bar, lamp posts), `lamp` (unshaded at night).

## Flat Cityscape convention and road ownership

The junctions sit right at both ends of the bridge, so the ramps cannot start on the approach roads as on the Pont Laviolette:

- **South**: the southbound exit to Boulevard du Saguenay Ouest (26227419) leaves 34 m south of the mapped south end, and beyond it the curved viaduct over Boulevard du Saguenay continues to Boulevard Saint-Paul. The alignment starts 35 m beyond the south end (1 m before the exit's gore; the gore is 0.003 m above grade).
- **North**: Route 172 and its ramps pass under the Route 175 from 4.6 m beyond the mapped north end (the Route 175's own overpass, mapped 1387995465/49827422, then the northbound exit 83 m out). The alignment ends 2 m past the mapped north end, before them.
- So both ramps climb over the end spans of the bridge itself: a cubic ease from each foot (zero grade) to a knee (70 m / 3.3 m south, 60 m / 3.0 m north), a constant grade, and a 160 m crest curve at 12.0 m. **Ramp lengths ~185 m (south) and ~190 m (north) to the crest curve; steepest grade 6.7 %.** The flat-map deck is 2.75 m at P1, 5.85 m at P2, 9.9-12.0-10.5 m at P3-P5, 6.7 m at P6, 2.8 m at P7; the steel box gets shallower (bottom ≥ 0.3 m) where the ramps near grade and ends 15 m short of the north end. Beyond the alignment the provider draws the south viaduct and the north overpass as usual.
- Ownership: the shared corridor (deck half-width + 1.5 m); carriageway edges [-8.0, -0.45] and [0.45, 8.0]. `bridgeRoadHeight` refuses crossing headings and the sidewalks.

### Shared change: `clipStandardEnds` (bridge-roads.js)

In the local app the provider (standard, no HD) draws the northbound carriageway as **one straight 531 m segment** from the south end to the far end of the Route 172 overpass (Protomaps simplification). `prepareStandard` removed a triangle only when all its points lie on the alignment, so that segment stayed drawn on the water under the deck (a white road under the bridge). The fix is an optional spec flag: with `clipStandardEnds: true` `prepareStandard` passes `clipEnds` to `_prepare`, the same plane split at the alignment ends that the HD path already uses (barycentric weights keep every attribute; for a ribbon aligned with the bridge the split in centre space matches the extruded ribbon). Absent, nothing changes: the Montréal, Paris, Prince Edward Viaduct, Champlain and bridge-layer tests pass unchanged (154 tests), and `pont-dubuc.test.js` proves both the split with the flag and the unchanged whole-segment behaviour without it.

## Full 3D world: the real deck

`terrainPolicy: 'absolute-deck'` (no corridor flattening). `layer.js` tags the surface once the DEM is known and the profile returns the **same shape** between the terrain roads at the alignment ends (4.5 m south, 7.9 m north in the app, plus 0.6 m eased in over 25 m) and 16.0 m above sea level over the river (12.0 m + the DEM's 4 m river). Piers and ramp walls keep their feet on the DEM (`bridgeLift` 0); the piers stretch proportionally. Real grades: 8.7 % at most (the south ramp foot), 2-6 % on the bridge.

Limits: the real deck at both bridge ends is higher than this profile (the south end continues on the viaduct over Boulevard du Saguenay, the north end over Route 172), but the provider's roads beyond the alignment are drawn at their own (terrain) heights, so the ramps must meet them there. The DEM is coarse (~30 m lattice, integer metres).

## Approximations and not modelled

Pier positions and the span split are estimated (none is mapped; seven piers and seven spans are published, the interior spans read equal in photographs). The deck height is estimated from photographs; the vertical profile is a flat-map compromise and a real-terrain shape of the same form. The curved concrete viaduct south of the bridge (over Boulevard du Saguenay) and the Route 175 overpass over Route 172 are the provider's, not modelled. The box is a plain trapezoid (no stiffeners or bearings), brackets and members are plain boxes, the piers have no footings or cofferdams, lamp heads are boxes; drainage, expansion joints, signage and the sign gantry at the north end are not modelled. The flat-map piers near both ends are short and the box gets shallower at the ramp feet.
