# Pont Laviolette (`pont-laviolette`)

The A-55 crossing of the St. Lawrence between Trois-Rivières and Bécancour, opened on 20 December 1967: the only bridge over the river between Montréal and Québec and the longest steel structure in Québec. A continuous turquoise steel truss carries the four-lane deck through 1.4 km; over the navigation channel its top chords sweep up into a through arch (crown 106.6 m) whose lower chords dip through the deck to pin bearings on the two main piers, so the arch and its anchor spans read as one S-curved silhouette. Long girder approaches on twin-column piers climb from both shores. A **road-fitted bridge**: the deck, car, route, camera and HD pavement share one station/lateral/height profile built on the two mapped A-55 carriageways.

Files: `src/peregrine/landmarks/quebec/pont-laviolette/` (`config.js`, `footprint.js`, `geometry.js`, `views.js`, `layer.js`, `pont-laviolette-profile.js`, `-structure.js`, `-alignment.js` generated by `-derive.mjs`, `pont-laviolette.test.js`), `prototypes/assets3d/catalog.d/pont-laviolette.json`, exports `public/models/bridges/pont-laviolette{-near,-far}.glb` + `.json`. ## Modelling

- **Truss planes** (`geometry.js`): every chord, vertical and diagonal is an open box member merged per material and chunk. Warren web in the through-truss spans, X web where the truss is deeper than 20 m (over the main piers), N-pattern diagonals rising towards the crown across the arch. A deck-level floor-line girder runs in each plane wherever the lower chord leaves the floor.
- **Arch**: top chord a parabola from 105.8 m (centre) to 78.5 m over the main piers, then a Hermite ease to the through-truss top chord over 128 m of each anchor span; lower chord a parabola from 96.3 m to the 27.5 m bearings, crossing the deck at the cantilever tips; the anchor spans' lower chords climb back from the bearings to the floor over 105 m (the V at each main pier).
- **Hangers**: 23 rope hangers per plane (the panel points between the tips) from the lower chord to the floor line.
- **Bracing**: top struts and top laterals at every panel; knee-braced sway frames in the through trusses with their feet 7.5 m above the road; deep portals at both truss ends; struts and X frames between the arch lower chords where they are 7 m or more above the road; struts and X frames under the deck where the chords dip to the main piers.
- **Deck**: asphalt, a central concrete wall and two outer barriers (crisp-edged solids), the floor system between the trusses, the slab and three plate girders (2.9 m) or the precast girders (1.45 m) on the approaches; lamps on the outer barriers outside the trusses and hung from the top struts inside them; red obstruction lights at the crown.
- **Piers**: twin rectangular concrete columns with a cap beam: massive (6.5 × 5 m) under the main-pier bearings, 3 × 3.4 m under the truss planes, 2 × 2.4 m under the approach girders.
- **Chunks**: five station chunks in near (north approach, north trusses, arch, south trusses, south approach) for culling; far is one chunk.
- Materials: `steel` (turquoise), `girder`, `floor`, `cable`, `concrete`, `asphalt`, `paint`, `lamp`, `light`.

## Flat Cityscape convention and road ownership

Roads pass under both ends of the bridge (mapped): the A-55 U-turn loop 67 m from the north end, Rue Notre-Dame Ouest (two carriageways, a footway and a cycleway) 126-154 m, a cycleway at 363 m, Rue du Pont at 535 m, a service road at 575-590 m, and Boulevard Bécancour 25 m from the south end. So the real abutments already stand on embankments, and on the flat map the ramps start on the approach roads:

- **North**: the alignment (and the ramp foot) starts 75 m beyond the north end, past the U-turn loop's junctions (71 and 83 m out; the loop is 0.01 m above grade there). A cubic ease (zero grade at the foot) reaches a knee 80 m inside the bridge 10.5 m up, then a constant 3.9 % grade to the 700 m parabolic crest curve centred on the arch (52.0 m). Steepest grade 9.3 %, 4.4 m at the north end, 9.9 m over the U-turn loop, 12.6 m over Rue Notre-Dame.
- **South**: the foot 115 m beyond the south end (the next join, a service crossover, is 128 m out), a knee 50 m inside the bridge 9.0 m up, 3.7 % grade. 6.2 m at the south end, 7.8 m over Boulevard Bécancour.
- Every road under the approaches clears the girders by at least 4.8 m on the flat map (test), and the piers stand between them.
- Beyond the abutments the ramps run on embankments between retaining walls (solid concrete wedges whose base stays on the ground, `bridgeLift` 0).
- Ownership: the shared corridor (deck half-width + 4 m); carriageway edges [-7.55, -0.35] and [0.35, 7.55]. `bridgeRoadHeight` refuses crossing headings, so the roads under the bridge never read the deck.

## Full 3D world: the real deck

`terrainPolicy: 'absolute-deck'` (no corridor flattening). `layer.js` tags the surface once the DEM is known and the profile returns the **same shape** between the terrain roads at the alignment ends (DEM ~10 m on both terraces, plus 0.6 m eased in over 25 m so the DEM lattice cannot show through the pavement) and 58.0 m at the crest (52.0 m + 6.0 m high water, the DEM's river level). The GLB is one model for both modes: the deck and structure move with the profile; pier feet (`bridgeLift` 0) stay on the DEM. Real grades: 9.4 % at most (the ramps), about 3.4 % on the bridge.

## Approximations and not modelled

Pier positions are estimated (none is mapped): the main span is placed by the published span lengths from the mapped north end, the north approach piers between the roads that pass under it. The vertical profile is estimated apart from the published crest height (and the 49.4 m clearance it satisfies); the real embankment heights at the ends are not known, so Full 3D world uses the flat-map knee heights above the terrain roads. Members are plain boxes (no lacing, gussets or pins); rope hangers are thin boxes; the truss pier columns are prismatic (no taper); the protection islands around the main piers, the deck drainage, the lane-control gantries, signage and the architectural lighting are not modelled. The flat-map ramps (9.3 %) are steeper than the real approach embankments. Lane paint is a fallback that the HD paint replaces.
