# Calgary Central Library

Landmark id `calgary-central-library`. Original procedural model of the Calgary Central Library, 800 3 Street SE, in its current form (opened 1 November 2018): Snøhetta with DIALOG's four-storey, pointed-ellipse building, a white aluminium wall with a crystalline pattern of hexagon-derived glazed and silver panels, a steam-bent western red cedar arch over a terraced entrance plaza on its west side, a lifted north prow over the stone portal where the CTrain South Line emerges from its tunnel, and a skylit atrium seen from above as a raised oculus.

Source: `src/peregrine/landmarks/calgary/calgary-central-library/` (`config.js`, `footprint.js`, `geometry.js`, `views.js`, `calgary-central-library-site.js`, `calgary-central-library-mesh.js`, `calgary-central-library.test.js`). Build: `pnpm build:calgary-landmarks calgary-central-library`. Exports: `public/models/buildings/calgary-central-library{-near,-far}.glb` and `.json`. Contract: [3d-calgary-landmarks.md](../calgary-landmarks.md).

## Frame

Real metres, +X east, +Y up, +Z south. Origin `[-114.0550316, 51.0454]` is the area centroid of OSM way 496824026. `y = 0` is street grade: 3 Street SE at the west facade and the CTrain tracks at the north-west of the prow. The building is not on the street grid: its long axis bears about 7 degrees and its outline is a pointed ellipse, which is the rotation; it is baked into the geometry from the mapped outline (the layer must not rotate the model). `calgary-central-library-site.js` turns the 24 mapped nodes into a 23-vertex ring that starts at the north tip and runs down the curved west side (OSM node 1, a 0.5 m jog, is dropped), corner-cuts the smooth stretches twice (so the wall is a curve inside the outline) and keeps five sharp corners (the tip, the south-west and south-east corners and the two ends of the 1.9 m notch on the east side). The wall stands 0.25 m inside the mapped outline.

## Materials

`panel` (white aluminium wall, parapet inner face), `silver` (silver panels, coping, mullions), `glass` (clear glazed cells), `frit` (fritted cells), `cedar` and `cedarDark` (the soffit in eight alternating bands, and the arch's end reveals), `stone` (the portal wall and the plaza treads), `roof`, `glow` (the ground-floor glazing and the oculus: unshaded, a day glass colour that turns warm at night) and `tunnel` (the near-black opening). Light and dark palettes share the keys. Far uses 7 materials: `silver` folds into `panel`, `frit` into `glass`, `cedarDark` into `cedar`.

## Modelling decisions

- The wall is one merged white surface to the 20.4 m coping, with the panels as separate quads laid on it. The hexagon-derived "tumbling blocks" tiling (each hexagon is three rhombi meeting at its centre) is laid on the unrolled wall by arc length and height; each rhombus is drawn only if it clears the arch's lower edge, the roofline and every sharp corner, then chosen glazed (probability from eight Gaussian clusters: the great cluster over the arch, the full-height south-west corner, over the portal, and scattered ones), silver or left as bare wall. Skirts close each panel's sides only where it stands higher than its neighbour, so no two panels share a face. No box per cell.
- The arch is a function of arc length on the wall line: facade lower edge, recess depth, terrace height. The cedar soffit is a dished surface (alternating bands) from the facade's lower edge to the glazing line; vertical reveals close its two ends where the arch meets the ground. The soffit stays level over its outer half (curve f^2.2) and climbs only toward the glazing, so most of it is visible from 40 m at street level (a steeply rising soffit hides behind the lip). The arch is tall (10 m lip, 13.6 m deep at the peak) so that it reads as the library's signature in street views and in the end-on reveals.
- The plaza terraces follow the arch height too: the stepped stone fades out where the lower edge drops under about 6.5 to 9 m, so the stone slab is narrower than the arch and no longer reads as a pale band along the whole west side.
- Where the recess closes to nothing at the ends of the prow wedge the cedar strips are near-vertical; the glazing there is set 2 cm behind the recess line (`GLOW_BIAS`) so that no cedar face shares its plane.
- The prow cannot use an offset recess: the building is only 4.6 m wide 4.4 m from its tip, so an offset line would loop. The lifted wedge is closed instead by a straight portal wall, square to the tunnel, where the wedge is 21 m wide (the tunnel passes 7.4 m from the east wall, so the opening sits off-centre with a wide stone pier on the north-west side). The soffit is a fan of quads from the wedge's wall line to the chord. The stone wall, glazing band, mullions and a closed dark tunnel box follow the chord.
- Everything stays inside the outline (worst vertex 0.03 m outside in near, none in far), including the plaza: the terraces rise inside the recess under the arch, not beyond the facade line. The at-grade tracks leaving the tunnel to the north-west are 10 m or more outside the outline and stay clear.
- Far keeps the same wall, arch profile, prow, portal and oculus with a 5.6 m ring instead of 2.6 m, two soffit bands on the same curve as near (so the arch stays readable), a ramp for the terraces, no mullions, no skirts and no silver or fritted cells.

## Approximations and what is left out

The arch, the prow and the portal are built from four photographs; the real portal is a 10 m grey board-formed concrete box and the real arch is a double-curved shell, not a dished strip. The facade is a vertical wall (no lean, no seams); the pattern is a deterministic estimate, not the real panel schedule. The tunnel box is straight and 14 m deep (the real tunnel curves south under the building). The region under the lifted prow in front of the portal wall is open. The mapped tunnel mouth lies about 10 m outside the building outline, which this model does not cover. Not modelled: the interior and the cedar atrium, the plaza beyond the facade line, the trains and rails, the sculpture, trees and planters, signage, the courtyard of the neighbouring Municipal Building. Cityscape and Full 3D world: not tested yet, integration is checked separately. The East Village site is expected to be nearly flat, so no `terrainPad` is declared; `padM` 86 covers the outline (79 m from the origin to the north tip). If the DEM varies by more than about 2 m under the plan, a `spec.terrainPad` is needed.

## Verification

Looked at (renders in `tmp/calgary/shots/calgary-central-library/`, ignored by git): near light overview, west facade, street level in front of the arch, under the vault, prow and portal, south-west street view at 100 m, east flank, north, roof, top view over the red outline; near dark overview, street, portal and roof; far light overview, street, north and facade; far dark overview, street, north and roof; the exported GLBs near light (overview, facade, vault, portal, street, east, north, roof), near dark and far light. Compared against: `Central Library - Calgary Public Library.jpg` (west facade, arch, panel pattern), `Calgary Central Library, Canada; October 2019 (cropped).jpg` (arch, south wall, plaza steps), `20181109 Calgary Central Library with Ctrain.jpg` (portal, soffit and prow), `-calgary -alberta -centrallibrary (46158048075).jpg` (atrium oculus). Changed because of them: the first render's glass cells were dark and dense and a flat blue slab filled the arch (glass lightened, density and hexagon size reduced, silver share cut from 42 to 24 %, mullions added); the cedar soffit was a thin line (recess deepened to 12 m, soffit made a dished level surface, eight alternating bands); the portal's stone block ended in a ramp (vertical step) and the first prow, an offset recess, collapsed into a self-intersecting loop near the tip (replaced by the straight portal wall); a plain roof edge gained a coping.

After the independent review (recognisability 3/5: the arch read as a small brown wedge and the terraces as a pale slab wider than the arch) the arch lip was raised to 10 m (4 m at the westernmost node, 6 m at node 2) with the recess deepened to 13.6 m and the soffit rising 4.2 m toward the glazing, the far LOD got two soffit bands, and the terraces were limited to the high part of the arch (34 m). Looked at again: the `qa-sheet` of the exported GLBs (near and far), then near-light `arch`, `street`, `vault` and `facade` and far-light `street` and `vault` renders (`tmp/calgary/shots/fix-east-village/`): the brown arch and its end reveal now read from street level and in far, the stone is a lens-shaped plaza under the arch peak.
