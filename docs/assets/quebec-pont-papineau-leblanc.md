# Pont Papineau-Leblanc (`pont-papineau-leblanc`)

The A-19 crossing of the Rivière des Prairies between Montréal (Ahuntsic) and Laval, opened in 1969: one of the first cable-stayed bridges in North America and the first with its towers in the roadway median. A rust-brown weathering-steel box girder with an orthotropic deck carries six lanes over a 241 m main span and two 90 m side spans; two single square steel pylons rise 38.4 m above the deck in the median, each holding a fan of two stays a side in the central plane, on round concrete piers in the river. The freeway ends abruptly south of the bridge at Boulevard Henri-Bourassa. A **road-fitted bridge**: the deck, car, route, camera and HD pavement share one station/lateral/height profile built on the two mapped A-19 carriageways.

Files: `src/peregrine/landmarks/quebec/pont-papineau-leblanc/` (`config.js`, `footprint.js`, `geometry.js`, `views.js`, `layer.js`, `pont-papineau-leblanc-profile.js`, `-structure.js`, `-alignment.js` generated by `-derive.mjs`, `pont-papineau-leblanc.test.js`), `prototypes/assets3d/catalog.d/pont-papineau-leblanc.json`, exports `public/models/bridges/pont-papineau-leblanc{-near,-far}.glb` + `.json`. ## Modelling

- **Pylons** (`geometry.js`): a 1.8 m square near-black chocolate column (its own `pylon` material, sRGB 55/42/36: the photographs show the masts much darker than the rust-brown deck) from inside the box girder through the median to 38.4 m above the road, a shallow cap, a red obstruction light, and two anchorage bands where the fans leave it (near).
- **Stays**: eight stays, straight merged members (8 triangles each). Near draws each as two 0.32 m cables 0.9 m apart either side of the median (the pairs photograph 1 shows); far as one 0.9 m member in the axis. Their deck ends sit in the median wall under a steel anchorage block.
- **Deck**: asphalt (0.25 m), the orthotropic deck plate cantilevered to the curbs, fascia edge girders, the two-cell trapezoidal box with sloped webs (lofted along the profile), cantilever ribs every 4.75 m under the overhangs (near), four shallow plate girders on the end spans; low concrete curbs with a steel rail on posts every 6 m (near), a raised median with a central wall; lamps on both curbs every 38 m (near), clear of the stays and pylons.
- **Supports**: round main piers (12-gon near, 8-gon far) rising from the river and flaring into a cap under the box, a footing (near); wall end piers under the box; abutment seats at the mapped bridge ends; beyond them the flat-map ramps run on retaining-wall embankments (solid wedges whose base stays on the ground, `bridgeLift` 0).
- **Chunks**: three station chunks in near (Laval ramp and north side span, main span, south side span and Montréal ramp); far is one chunk.
- Materials: `steel` and `girder` (weathering steel; far folds the girder into steel), `pylon`, `cable`, `concrete`, `asphalt`, `paint`, `pole`, `lamp`, `light`.

## Flat Cityscape convention and road ownership

Junctions (mapped) near the ends, checked before choosing the ramps:

- **Laval**: the northbound exit to Boulevard Lévesque leaves 90 m beyond the bridge end; the southbound entrance from it merges 122 m beyond; Boulevard Lévesque crosses over the A-19 at 111 m. A private service track passes under the Laval end span 3–6 m inside the bridge. So the ramp foot is 85 m beyond the end, short of the exit gore: a 30 m vertical curve from grade 0, a constant **7.2 %** grade, and a 40 m crest curve that reaches 11.3 m just past the north pylon. The deck is 5.1 m above grade at the bridge end and 5.4 m over the service track (3.2 m under the end-span girders).
- **Montréal**: nothing joins the A-19 until Boulevard Henri-Bourassa, 684 m beyond the bridge end, where the freeway ends. The ramp foot is 120 m beyond the end, still on the level riverside terrace (so Full 3D world takes the foot from flat ground): a 60 m curve after the south pylon, a constant **6.9 %** grade, a 50 m curve to grade 0. 6.6 m at the bridge end, 7.5 m over the riverside cycleway (3.8 m under the box).
- Across the main span the deck is level at 11.3 m, the box bottom at 7.6 m (the published clearance; tested by raycast).
- Ownership: the shared corridor (deck half-width + 4 m); carriageway edges [−12.75, −1.5] and [1.5, 12.75]. `bridgeRoadHeight` refuses crossing headings, so the exit, the entrance, the service track, the cycleway and Boulevard Lévesque never read the deck (tests and in-app).

## Full 3D world: the real deck

`terrainPolicy: 'absolute-deck'` (no corridor flattening). `layer.js` tags the surface once the DEM is known, and the profile returns the real deck on the **same stations** (the same curves and grade breaks): from the terrain road at the Laval foot (DEM ~32.6 m: the A-19 comes down the bluff, so the real deck descends onto the bridge, at 2.8 % at most) to 28.2 m above sea level across the main span (11.3 m over the 16.9 m river) and down at 5.5 % at most to the Montréal terrace (19.8 m), plus 0.6 m eased in over 25 m so the DEM lattice cannot show through the pavement. The GLB is one model for both modes: deck and structure move with the profile; pier and wall feet (`bridgeLift` 0) stay on the DEM.

## Approximations and not modelled

No pier is mapped: the cable-stayed unit is centred on the mapped bridge (1.5 m towards Montréal), the end spans fill the rest. The stay anchor stations, the pairing of each stay, the box section, the pier and abutment shapes and the vertical profile (apart from the published deck height and clearance) are estimated; the real approach embankments are unknown. Members are plain boxes (no cable sag, sockets, saddles or bearings); expansion joints, drainage, signage, the overhead sign gantries at both ends and the architectural lighting are not modelled. The flat-map ramps (7.2 %) are steeper than the real approaches. Lane paint is a fallback that the HD paint replaces.
